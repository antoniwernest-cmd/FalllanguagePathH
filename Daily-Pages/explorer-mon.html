Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Aquarium Day

Mira and Jonah planned to visit the river fish exhibit at the forest aquarium. A paper map showed a route through the east hallway, but a cleaning barrier stood across its entrance. Mira did not step past it. She checked for a current direction. A new blue sign pointed toward the west hall. The map still helped her understand where the halls met, although it could not show the temporary closure. They followed the west hall past displays of water plants. Jonah expected to see fish at once. Instead, the next aquarium looked still. They checked the sign again and walked around a bend. A wide exhibit held smooth stones with narrow gaps between them. At first they saw only moving shadows. Then small fish came out from the stones and crossed the open water. Jonah recognised the habitat shown on a poster near the entrance. The poster had helped him know what to look for; it had not told him which hallway to use. On their way out, Mira listed the clues in the order they had mattered. The barrier showed that the east route was unavailable. The new blue sign supplied another direction. The stones and fish confirmed that they had reached the exhibit they wanted. She could explain their choice without claiming that the old map was wrong about the whole building. It described a route that had worked before the cleaning. If they returned later, they would read the current signs again because the open route could change.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Aquarium Day

At the river fish exhibit, Jonah noticed small fish crossing the open part of the aquarium. A keeper lowered the lights for an evening display. Almost at once, the fish moved toward a cluster of smooth stones. Soon the open water looked empty. Jonah wondered whether the keeper had taken the fish away. Mira stayed by the glass and looked at the gaps between the stones. She saw a tail move, then another. As the lights dimmed, the small fish slipped behind the rocks. A display card said that the spaces among the stones offered shelter. One fish emerged briefly but turned back when a larger fish passed. These details supported the idea that several small fish were still in the exhibit. They did not prove that every fish had moved for the same reason. The light, the larger fish, and the available hiding places could each matter. Jonah revised his first thought in his notebook. He wrote what changed, what the fish did, and what Mira could still see. The open water had become quiet, yet the moving tails showed that the fish had not vanished. Mira wanted to watch again during a brighter display before making a broader claim about their behaviour. For now, the strongest inference was limited to the fish sheltering behind the stones at that time. A careful observer could point to the movement in the gaps and explain why an apparently empty exhibit was still home to the fish.

Which detail best supports this inference? Several small fish were probably sheltering behind the stones when the lights dimmed.
