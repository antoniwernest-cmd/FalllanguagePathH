Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Boating Day

Mira and Jonah reached the forest lake carrying a brochure that pictured boats at the exposed dock. The wind that afternoon was stronger than it had been when the photograph was taken. Waves struck the outer posts, and a barrier closed the exposed dock. The friends stayed behind it. A current notice pointed to a sheltered boating dock. It directed visitors along a shore path. They read the notice before choosing which way to walk. The shore path first ran beside open water, where spray reached the rocks. Mira and Jonah kept to the marked walking area. Around a bend, trees blocked much of the wind. The water there looked calmer, and several boats rested beside the sheltered dock. Jonah checked that the dock named on the notice matched the place in front of them. They had found the boats without using the closed dock or assuming that boating had stopped everywhere on the lake. The brochure was still useful for showing what visitors usually found at the lake. It could not report the day’s wind or a temporary closure. The barrier and current notice gave more timely information, while the boats at the sheltered dock confirmed their destination. Mira could say why they chose that route and what they had actually observed. She could not promise that the sheltered dock would stay open in every kind of weather. Before another visit, they would check the latest notice and conditions again.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Boating Day

Mira and Jonah reached a path above the forest lake while the water beside the dock still looked smooth. A small flag on a post hung almost straight down. As they watched, the flag lifted and the reeds began to sway. The first ripples spread outward, then the water beyond the reeds became rougher. The friends remained on the shore path while they compared the changes. The dock flag snapped faster as the lake ripples became choppier. Jonah thought the wind was strengthening at that part of the lake. Mira looked at the leaves along the shore; they moved in the same direction as the flag. A notice advised visitors to check current conditions before taking a boat out. The two decided to wait and ask dock staff for an update. They had several signs of changing weather, but no instrument reading that gave an exact wind speed. From a sheltered bend, the water looked calmer. Mira did not let that view erase what they had seen at the exposed dock. The lake could have different conditions in different places. In her notes, she named the location and the order of observations: still flag, faster movement, growing ripples, then choppier water. That record supported a careful inference about wind near the dock. It did not show how long the wind would last or prove that every part of the lake was equally rough.

Which detail best supports this inference? The wind was probably becoming stronger at the exposed dock.
