Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Bike Ride Day

Mira and Jonah set out on the woodland bike trail with a printed route to the lookout. Rain had fallen overnight. At the first fork, deep ruts cut across the lower path shown on their map. A bicycle wheel could slip into one of them. Fresh orange arrows marked a higher path. Mira stopped to check where the arrows led before they moved their bicycles. The upper path curved through pines and stayed inside the marked riding area. Its soil felt firm beneath their shoes, so they walked their bicycles uphill. Near the top, loose gravel made the slope harder to climb. Mira slowed down and kept both hands on her handlebars. From one bend, they could still see the muddy lower trail below them. They did not need to test the ruts to know that the lower route was a poor choice that morning. Around the final turn, the trees opened and the lookout came into view. At the lookout, Jonah compared the printed route with the path they had taken. The paper still showed where the lower trail went, but it could not show how rain had changed its surface. The fresh arrows offered a route, and the firm ground gave the pair another reason to use it. They had observed the mud, checked the marked path, and reached the lookout safely. On a drier day, the lower trail might be usable again; the condition of a route can change.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Bike Ride Day

Halfway through their forest ride, Jonah noticed that steering felt heavier than it had at the start. He stopped at a rest bench and pressed the front tire. It bent more than the rear one. Mira listened as he turned the wheel and heard a faint hiss near the valve. They checked the rubber surface first and saw no sharp object. Then Jonah found that the valve had been left loose after the tire was filled that morning. They tightened the valve, used the pump at the rest stop, and waited. The front tire stayed firm through a slow test ride on level ground. Mira wrote down the order of their checks because a soft tire could have more than one cause. The hiss near the valve was a useful clue, but the tire holding air after the valve was secured gave them stronger evidence for the explanation. At the next trail sign, they stopped once more. The tire still felt firm. Jonah did not claim that a loose valve always causes a soft tire; a puncture would require a different repair. In this case, however, their observation, the small change they made, and the result supported a reasonable conclusion about what had happened.

Which detail best supports this inference? The loose valve was probably letting air out of Jonah’s front tire.
