Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Cave Explore Day

Mira and Jonah followed the forest cave trail hoping to see the limestone wall. Their old map led toward the lower tunnel, but a barrier showed that the tunnel was closed to visitors. Cool air drifted from its entrance. New reflective markers led to an upper walkway. The markers were easy to see even where the cave grew dim. They stayed within the visitor area and climbed to the walkway. A firm rail ran beside the path, while the closed tunnel remained below. Around a bend, the cave grew dim enough that the next turn was hard to see. Mira waited until a reflective marker caught the light, then followed it to the rail ahead. From the upper walkway, the pale limestone wall came into view. Thin lines of mineral colour crossed its surface. They saw the wall without entering the tunnel or guessing where an unmarked passage might lead. At the exit, Jonah unfolded the old map. It explained why they had first looked toward the lower tunnel, but it could not show the barrier or the newly marked route. The reflective markers matched the path that was open that day. Mira could point to the barrier, the markers, and the walkway as evidence for their decision. A cave route might change after maintenance, so they would check the current markers again on another visit.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Cave Explore Day

Inside the forest cave’s visitor area, Mira heard water tapping against stone. She looked down and saw drops falling onto one step beside the marked walkway. That step had a shiny surface, while the nearby steps appeared dry. Jonah wondered whether the shine came from a mineral in the stone rather than water. They watched another drop land and spread across the surface. A visitor guide had already placed a small caution marker near the step. Mira pointed out the dripping water, and the guide directed visitors to remain on the dry, marked side of the walkway. The friends followed the instruction. They did not try to test the wet step with their shoes. From where they stood, they could describe the drops, the sheen, and the guide’s response without inventing a story about a hidden stream. In their notes, Jonah wrote that the wet step might be slippery. That was an inference based on its surface and the caution marker, not something he had tested directly. Mira added the exact observation that supported it: water was falling on the step and spreading over the stone. The pair continued along the marked route and saw the cave display safely. They left the reason for the dripping water to the guide and the cave team to investigate.

Which detail best supports this inference? The wet cave step might be slippery.
