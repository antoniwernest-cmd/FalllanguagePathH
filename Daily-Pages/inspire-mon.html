Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Aquarium Day

Mira and Jonah reached the forest aquarium eager to see the river fish exhibit. Their map sent them toward the east hall, but a cleaning barrier blocked that hallway. The map did not show another way. A new blue sign pointed toward the west hall. Mira noticed that the sign stood near the entrance, where visitors could see it before reaching the barrier. Several families were already walking west. Beside the sign, a river-habitat poster showed fish sheltering beneath smooth stones. The poster reminded Jonah what they hoped to see, but it did not say which hallway was open. They followed the west corridor past a display of water plants. For a moment, Jonah thought they might have gone too far because the river fish were nowhere in sight. Around a bend, Mira spotted a wide tank filled with smooth stones. She waited until something flickered between them. Small fish darted into the open water, then returned to the gaps. They had reached the exhibit without crossing the closed hall. On the way out, they looked at the map and sign side by side. The map was useful for finding the aquarium, yet it had been printed before the cleaning work. The sign described what visitors could do that day. Mira had also seen people using the west hall, which supported the direction on the sign. She did not assume the east hall would remain closed forever. If they came back another day, they would check the current signs before choosing a route.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Aquarium Day

Mira and Jonah visited the aquarium’s river fish exhibit before the keeper dimmed its lights for the evening display. Several small fish crossed the open water in front of the glass. As the lights dimmed, the fish slipped behind the rocks. Soon the open part of the display looked almost empty. Jonah wondered if the fish had been moved elsewhere. Mira looked closely along the bottom and could still see a few tails moving in the gaps. A card beside the display explained that the rocks gave the fish places to shelter, but it did not say that every fish would hide whenever the light changed. The pair watched for several minutes. One fish came out briefly, then turned back when a larger fish passed. Their observation supported a narrower idea: many of the small fish were using the rocks at that moment. It did not prove why every individual fish moved. Before leaving, Jonah wrote down what they had seen in order. First the lights changed; next the fish moved toward the stones; then the open water looked quiet. Recording that sequence helped him explain why the display seemed empty without claiming that the fish had vanished. He planned to look again during the brighter morning display to see whether their positions changed.

Which detail best supports this inference? Several small fish were probably sheltering behind the stones when the lights dimmed.
