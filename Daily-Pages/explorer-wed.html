Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Tractors Day

At a forest-edge farm, Mira and Jonah wanted to reach the seed garden. The farm map showed a narrow footpath beside a field. A parked tractor now stood across that path, and its tires filled much of the lane. The friends stopped well back from the machine. A fresh walking-route sign pointed around the field. It led along a fence. Mira checked that it marked a visitor path, then they set off on foot. The fence path led past rows of newly planted seedlings. Near a gate, another walking marker confirmed that visitors should stay on the outer side of the lane. Jonah could see the tractor through the fence, but the new route kept people away from the machinery area. The gate was open for walkers, and the seed garden appeared beyond it. They reached the garden without trying to squeeze past the tractor. Mira examined the map again after their visit. It was useful for locating the garden, yet it had no way to show a tractor parked on the footpath that day. The walking signs described the route available to visitors, and each marker matched the clear path they could see. Jonah did not conclude that the whole farm was closed or unsafe. Only the narrow footpath had been blocked. If the tractor moved later, the old route might open, but they would still follow the current visitor signs on another visit.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Tractors Day

At the forest-edge farm, two sets of wheel marks crossed a soft lane near the shed. The older marks looked pale and firm. The other marks had damp soil piled along their sides. A tractor stood nearby, and mud clung to its tires. Jonah thought the tractor must have made the damp tracks, but Mira asked what the marks themselves could tell them. The newer wheel marks cut through soft soil and still had loose edges. They appeared to have been made after the ground became wet. A farm notice mentioned that supplies had been moved after a shower, which made a recent trip plausible. The friends could not tell from the marks alone who drove a vehicle, exactly when it crossed, or whether the nearby tractor made that particular set of tracks. Other farm machines could leave marks too. Mira wrote a limited inference: a heavy vehicle had probably crossed the lane after the rain softened the soil. She listed the damp, loose edges as evidence and wrote the tractor’s muddy tires as a separate observation. Later, a farm worker might be able to identify the vehicle, but the friends had not witnessed its movement. Jonah revised his first claim so it did not go beyond what they could check. Their note distinguished an observation from a likely explanation and left the uncertain details open.

Which detail best supports this inference? A heavy vehicle probably crossed the farm lane after the soil became wet.
