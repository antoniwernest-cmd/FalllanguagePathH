Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Tractors Day

Mira and Jonah arrived at the forest-edge farm to visit the seed garden. Their map showed a narrow footpath beside the field, but a parked tractor blocked it. Deep tire marks crossed the lane. The friends stopped well away from the machinery and looked for a visitor route. A fresh walking-route sign pointed around the field. They followed the sign along a fence where the path was clear. From there, Mira could see why the old map had been confusing: it showed the footpath but not the tractor standing on it that day. At a bend, the fence path appeared to end beside a gate. A small walking marker on the gate directed visitors away from the machinery lane and toward the garden. The friends stayed on the marked side, passed rows of seedlings, and reached the seed garden without walking near the tractor. Jonah looked back at the two possible routes. The old map was still useful for showing the farm layout, but it could not report a temporary obstruction. The walking sign gave a current direction, and the clear path around the fence matched what the sign showed. Their choice rested on things they could check: the blocked footpath, the fresh sign, and the open visitor path. If the tractor moved later, the route might change again.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Tractors Day

At the forest-edge farm, Mira studied two sets of wheel marks crossing the lane near a shed. One set was pale and dry. The other cut through soft soil where the ridges still crumbled at the edges. A tractor stood nearby with damp earth on its tires. Jonah guessed that the fresh marks had been made that morning, but Mira wanted to separate what they could see from what they merely suspected. The farm notice said machinery had moved supplies after a brief shower. That information fit the damp marks, yet it did not identify which machine had left each track. They could observe the width and depth of the marks, and they could see that the wet soil inside them had not dried like the older tracks. The pair recorded those details before choosing an explanation. Mira thought a heavy vehicle had probably crossed the lane more recently than the vehicle that made the pale marks. She could not prove the exact time or driver from tracks alone. Jonah added that the tractor’s muddy tires made it a plausible source, but another farm machine might have similar tires. Their field note stated the strongest claim the evidence supported and left the uncertain part open for the farmer to confirm.

Which detail best supports this inference? A heavy vehicle probably crossed the farm lane after the soil became wet.
