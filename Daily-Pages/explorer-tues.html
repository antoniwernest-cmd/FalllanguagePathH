Activity 9 · Question 9

Reading Detective

Ontario C2.6

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Identify the main idea and the details that support it.

Read a story and find what it tells you.

📚 Bike Ride Day

Mira and Jonah carried a printed bike-trail route to a lookout above the forest. Overnight rain had left the lower trail rutted and muddy. Their map still showed it as the direct path. At the fork, they found another clue. Fresh orange arrows marked a higher path. Jonah wanted to know whether that marked path was suitable for bicycles before they followed it. They checked that the arrows stood beside the designated trail and that the ground ahead was firm. The climb was slower than the lower route would have been on a dry day. Mira walked her bicycle around loose gravel at one bend. She did not leave the marked trail to avoid every bump. From the next turn, they could see the muddy ruts below; a wheel might have caught there. The orange arrows continued uphill through the pines. When the trees thinned, the friends reached the lookout and parked their bicycles on firm ground. Jonah compared their route with the printed map. The map showed the trail layout, but it did not describe the mud left by last night’s rain. The newer arrows offered another route, and the condition of the higher trail supported taking it. Mira wrote down what they had observed rather than saying that the lower path would never be usable. A future rider would need to check the ground and the current markers again. Their decision made sense for the trail as it was that morning.

Which sentence best tells the main idea of this story?

Activity 10 · Question 10

Inference Detective

Ontario C3.2

Attempts: 0

Curriculum focus: Use stated details and clues to make an inference about the story.

Use clues to work out what is likely.

📚 Bike Ride Day

Jonah stopped his bicycle beside a forest rest bench because the front wheel no longer rolled as smoothly as it had earlier. The tire felt soft when he pressed it. Mira looked across the rubber for a thorn or a cut, but she saw neither. She turned the wheel slowly while Jonah listened. The sound seemed to come from the valve rather than the tread. A faint hiss came from the valve while the front tire felt soft. Jonah remembered filling that tire before the ride. He checked the valve and found it loose. After closing it carefully, he used the public pump beside the bench. They waited, pressed the tire again, and took a short, slow ride on level ground. It stayed firm. The result supported their explanation, although it did not prove that a tire could never have another problem. Mira recorded the checks in order. The soft tire showed that air had escaped. The hiss suggested a place to investigate. The loose valve supplied a possible cause, and the tire holding air after they closed it offered a further check. If the tire softened again, they would stop rather than keep riding and would look for a puncture or ask for help. Jonah did not turn one successful repair into a rule about all soft tires. Their conclusion applied to what they had observed on this ride.

Which detail best supports this inference? The loose valve was probably letting air out of Jonah’s front tire.
